01 — Introduction

Why Coincidence?

Nuclear experiments are drowned in background noise. These five demos show exactly why coincidence is the solution — from the atom up.

☢ LIVE SOURCE — Particles emitting in all directions. Only correlated hits register.
The radioactive source (☢) emits particles in all directions. Only particles hitting both Detector A and B within Δt are recorded as coincidences. Random background hits (cosmic rays) fire one detector only — and are silently rejected.
📉 OSCILLOSCOPE — Raw signal vs coincidence-filtered output
Without coincidence: the detector output is a storm of random spikes — cosmic rays, thermal noise, ambient radioactivity all pile on top of the real signal. With coincidence: only events where both detectors fire together survive. The noise floor collapses.

Why this works: A true physical event (e.g. a decaying nucleus) produces particles that travel through space and interact with both detectors nearly simultaneously. Background noise (random thermal spikes, cosmic rays hitting one detector) fires only one channel. The AND gate (coincidence unit) silently rejects single-channel events. Therefore, by requiring multiple detectors to fire within Δt, you automatically suppress random background by orders of magnitude while preserving real signals. This is the fundamental power of coincidence measurement — it gives physics a way to say "was this real?"

The Fundamental Tradeoff
When we try to reduce unwanted background signals, we also lose some real signals. So there is always a tradeoff:
More strict detection → less noise
But also → fewer real events detected

The Math (Simple Idea)
If we require more detectors to agree at the same time, random noise becomes very unlikely.

For example:
• If 2 detectors must agree → some noise is removed
• If 3 detectors must agree → much more noise is removed

But the problem is: Some real signals may not reach all detectors, so we lose some real data as well.

In simple terms: More strict conditions = cleaner data, but less data

03 — Coincidence Logic

Electronics & AND Gate Logic

The coincidence unit is fundamentally an electronic AND gate — it fires only when both inputs arrive within the resolving time window.

⚡ ANIMATED SIGNAL FLOW — Click to fire an event
🔀 Constant Fraction Discriminator (CFD)
A standard leading-edge discriminator triggers at a fixed voltage threshold — so large pulses trigger earlier than small ones. This timing walk degrades resolution.

The CFD triggers at a fixed fraction of the pulse peak (typically 20–30%), independent of amplitude. This achieves timing resolutions of ~20 ps FWHM.
🚫 Anti-Coincidence (Veto)
The inverse of coincidence — an event is rejected if a veto detector fires simultaneously.

Used to reject cosmic muons in underground detectors, bremsstrahlung events in beta spectroscopy, and Compton-scattered photons. Example: the COS-B satellite used anti-coincidence plastic scintillators to veto charged particle backgrounds in gamma-ray astronomy.
04 — Timing Techniques

Resolving Time & the TAC

Five interactive demos — explore every layer of coincidence timing from capacitor charging to full spectrum building.

🎬 TAC STEP-BY-STEP — Watch time convert to voltage
The TAC starts a voltage ramp when the START pulse (Det A) arrives. It charges a capacitor linearly with time. When the STOP pulse (Det B) arrives, charging halts — the final voltage height is proportional to the time difference Δt. This voltage is then digitised by an ADC into an MCA channel number.
⚡ TIMING WALK — Leading-edge vs CFD discriminator
Three pulses of different amplitudes hit the same detector. The threshold line (red dashes) is crossed at different times — causing timing walk. Switch to CFD: each pulse triggers at its own fixed fraction (30%) of peak — all trigger at the same relative time. Walk eliminated.
📊 LIVE SPECTRUM BUILDER — Click to fire events one by one
Each click fires real events sampled from the physics distributions. True coincidences cluster into a central Gaussian peak. Accidentals scatter uniformly across all channels. Watch the spectrum emerge exactly as it does in a real laboratory measurement.

Drag the slider to change Δt. Watch how the acceptance window (blue band) widens or narrows. A narrow Δt slices through some true coincidences (Gaussian clipped) — reducing efficiency. A wide Δt lets in more accidentals (flat background rises). The optimum is where peak/background ratio is maximised.
🔢 NIM vs FPGA — Old hardware vs modern digital timing
NIM / CAMAC HARDWARE
Discrete modules in a crate. Each stage (preamp → amp → CFD → TAC → ADC) is a separate physical box connected by coaxial cables. Reliable, well-understood — but bulky, expensive per channel, and limited to one time interval per START pulse.
FPGA DIGITAL TDC
All logic implemented in programmable firmware. Handles hundreds of channels simultaneously. Δt window is software-configurable in nanoseconds. Dead time approaches zero. Resolution ~13 ps demonstrated. Used in all modern large-scale experiments.
05 — Accidental Coincidences

True vs Accidental Coincidence

Not all coincidences are real. Understanding the accidental rate and how to subtract it is critical to any precision measurement.

✅ True Coincidence
Both signals originate from the same nuclear decay event. Physically correlated — they are causally linked. Rate depends on source activity and detector solid angle.
❌ Accidental Coincidence
Two unrelated events randomly fall within Δt by pure chance. Their rate is determined by singles rates and the resolving window — purely statistical.
Accidental Coincidence Rate Formula
RA = 2 · R1 · R2 · Δt
R_A — Accidental coincidence rate (s⁻¹)
R₁, R₂ — Singles rates in detectors 1 and 2
Δt — Resolving time window
Factor 2 — either detector can arrive first

🔧 Experimental Correction Method
To measure accidentals experimentally, introduce a known delay (≫ Δt) in one detector channel — typically several microseconds. Any coincidences recorded in this delayed window are purely accidental, since the physical correlation is destroyed.

Corrected true rate = R_prompt − R_delayed

This technique is used routinely in high-rate environments like safeguards neutron counting and activation analysis labs.
06 — Applications

Real-World Applications

Click each application to expand details. Coincidence measurements span from standard student lab experiments to frontier nuclear security and medical physics.

☢ γ–γ Angular Correlation
Two NaI detectors at variable angles measure cascade gamma rays from nuclear decay — determines spin and multipolarity of nuclear levels.
Nuclear StructureNaI DetectorsLab Experiment

In a typical experiment with ⁶⁰Co, two 1.17 MeV and 1.33 MeV cascade gammas are emitted. The angular correlation W(θ) ∝ 1 + A₂P₂(cosθ) + A₄P₄(cosθ) reveals the multipolarity and the angular momentum of the intermediate state. A coincidence spectrum is recorded at each angle.

⚛ β–γ Coincidence (Activity)
Absolute activity measurement of a radioactive source — avoids the need for calibrated sources or efficiency corrections by using geometric factors only.
MetrologyActivity Measurement⁶⁰Co

The measured coincidence rate R_c = A₀ · εβ · εγ, and singles rates Rβ = A₀·εβ, Rγ = A₀·εγ. Solving: A₀ = Rβ·Rγ / R_c. Efficiency cancels out — highly accurate absolute activity determination. Used in national metrology standards.

💫 Positron Lifetime Spectroscopy
TAC measures the lifetime of positronium formed in materials — provides direct information about electron density and defects at the atomic scale.
Materials ScienceTAC²²Na Source

Using a ²²Na source, the 1.27 MeV "birth" gamma (nearly simultaneous with e⁺ emission) starts the TAC. The 511 keV annihilation gamma stops it. The TAC spectrum yields the positronium lifetime (~125 ps for para-Ps, ~142 ns for ortho-Ps). Defect concentrations in semiconductors and polymers are directly probed.

⏰ Muon Lifetime Measurement
Classic student experiment: measure the muon mean lifetime (~2.197 µs) using scintillator coincidence and TAC — a direct test of special relativity.
Particle PhysicsSpecial RelativityStudent Lab

A muon enters a plastic scintillator (START pulse) and decays, producing a decay electron (STOP pulse). The TAC time spectrum shows an exponential decay: N(t) ∝ e−t/τ. Fitting gives τ_µ ≈ 2.197 µs, matching the PDG value precisely — validating relativistic time dilation since cosmic muons live long enough to reach Earth's surface only due to this effect.

🔒 Neutron Coincidence Counting
IAEA safeguards application: identify and quantify fissile materials (Pu, U) without destroying them — critical for nuclear non-proliferation.
IAEA SafeguardsNon-Destructive Assay³He Detectors

Spontaneous fission of ²⁴⁰Pu emits 2–3 neutrons nearly simultaneously. A shift-register coincidence circuit counts correlated neutron pairs within ~64 µs gate. The doubles rate is proportional to the ²⁴⁰Pu mass. Used in "AWCC" (Active Well Coincidence Counters) at nuclear fuel facilities worldwide.

🌟 Neutrino Discovery (1956)
The Cowan–Reines experiment used 4-fold coincidence to achieve the first confirmed detection of the neutrino — 26 years after Pauli proposed it.
HistoricalNobel Prize 19954-fold Coincidence

Anti-neutrinos from the Savannah River nuclear reactor induced inverse beta decay: ν̄_e + p → e⁺ + n. The positron annihilation produced two 511 keV gammas (prompt coincidence), and neutron capture produced gammas ~5 µs later (delayed coincidence). This 4-fold coincidence signature was essentially unique — signal-to-background ratio ~3:1. Reines received the Nobel Prize in 1995.

07 — Large Scale Physics

Coincidence at CERN's LHC

At the Large Hadron Collider, coincidence logic operates at a scale that would have been unimaginable to Bothe and Geiger in 1924.

40 MHz
Bunch Crossing Rate
Proton bunches collide every 25 nanoseconds. Each crossing can produce ~40 overlapping pp interactions (pile-up).
50 TB/s
Raw Data Rate
Recording every collision would produce 50 terabytes per second — exceeding the entire internet's bandwidth. Impossible to store or process.
~1 kHz
Final Storage Rate
After multi-level coincidence triggers, only ~1,000 events per second are written to permanent storage. A reduction of 40 million to one.

🔬 Higgs Boson: A Coincidence Triumph
Coincidence measurement was used in the Higgs discovery to reduce background noise. The detectors only recorded events when specific particles appeared at the same time, like two photons or four leptons. Random background signals almost never produce such exact combinations simultaneously. So by requiring coincidence, most of the noise was removed. This made it possible to clearly identify the real Higgs signal.
